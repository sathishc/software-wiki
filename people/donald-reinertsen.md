# Donald G. Reinertsen

Donald G. Reinertsen is a product development consultant and author whose
work reframed lean thinking for product development. Where lean was born in
manufacturing, Reinertsen argued that design work is different: it is
information-heavy, uncertain, and governed by queues and economics more than
by physical throughput.

## Key works

- *Developing Products in Half the Time* (1991, with Preston Smith) — an early
  treatment of speed as an economic lever in product development.
- *Managing the Design Factory* (1997) — treats product development as a
  production system whose output is information, with a focus on queues and
  inventory of work.
- *The Principles of Product Development Flow: Second Generation Lean Product
  Development* (2009) — the book synthesised in the wiki's
  [source notes](../sources/reinertsen-product-development-flow.md), presenting
  roughly 175 principles on flow.

## Core contribution

Reinertsen's lasting contribution is the **economic** argument for flow. He
insists that decisions should be made on the basis of value and
[cost of delay](../concepts/cost-of-delay.md), not on utilisation or cost per
unit of effort. From that starting point he derives the mechanics of
[queueing and utilisation](../concepts/queueing-and-utilization.md), the
case for [small batches](../concepts/batch-size.md), and the use of
[work-in-progress limits](../concepts/wip-limits.md) to control flow.

## Where he sits in the wiki

His ideas feed the [Lean Product Development](../pages/lean-product-development.md)
map. They also connect to the software-delivery work of
[Martin Fowler](./martin-fowler.md) and [Kent Beck](./kent-beck.md) on small
changes and fast feedback, and to the [DORA Metrics](../concepts/dora-metrics.md)
research that tested similar claims empirically in software.
