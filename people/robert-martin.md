# Robert C. "Uncle Bob" Martin

Robert C. Martin ("Uncle Bob") is the voice behind the **SOLID** principles,
the [Clean Code](../concepts/clean-code.md) movement, and a large part of the modern definition of
"good code." His influence is so wide it becomes a reference point for itself:
when someone says "clean code," they usually mean *his* clean code.

## Core contribution

- **SOLID** — the five single-letter design principles ([SOLID](../concepts/solid.md)) that are now
  taught in essentially every object-oriented course.
- **Clean Code** (*Clean Code*, 2008) — a practical handbook for readable,
  easy-to-change code: names, small functions, honest comments, clean error
  handling. See [Code Quality](../pages/code-quality.md).
- **The boy-scout rule** — "Always leave the campground cleaner than you found
  it." Leave every file you touch a little better than you found it.

## The recurring claim

**Readability is a feature.** Code is written once and read many times; the
cost of a confusing line is paid on every read. Clean code is not an aesthetic
preference, it is *economics* — the same logic as [Refactoring](../concepts/refactoring.md) and
[Technical Debt](../concepts/technical-debt.md).

## Where he fits

Martin sits at the **individual-contributor** end of the wiki. His ideas are
the atoms that [Code Review](../concepts/code-review.md) assembles into team culture and that
[Code Quality](../pages/code-quality.md) synthesizes into a practice. He is the person you point a
junior developer to first.
